OPTION A

Live clip

Your dino on a small test pad uses the part on dummies, in 3D, looping. This is how League of Legends, Valorant and Overwatch do it: a short clip per ability next to the text.

  • Looks the best and feels like the real game.
  • Most work: every part needs its own little scripted scene, and the real effects run on real characters, so each one has to be rebuilt to play inside the panel.
  • No numbers on it, so the chips do the explaining.
OPTION B · RECOMMENDED

Top-down diagram

A flat view from above, drawn from the part's real numbers: the cone, the dash, the cloud, rings marked in studs. Dummies react (knocked back, poisoned, silenced). Close to how Brawl Stars and MOBAs show reach.

  • About 6 shapes (cone, burst, dash, lob + cloud, ring, from-behind hit) cover every part.
  • Reads the same numbers the server uses, so it can't go stale when a part is tuned.
  • Clear on a phone; you see exact reach.
OPTION C

Stat bars + good at / watch out

Call of Duty's Gunsmith way: bars for Damage, Reach, Control and Uptime, plus a short "Good at" and "Watch out" list. No movement.

  • Least work and easy to compare two parts.
  • Doesn't show what the part does, only how strong it is, so it misses the ask.
ALL OPTIONS GET

One line + number chips

  • A one-sentence "what it does" in plain words above the steps.
  • Chips with the key numbers at the step you own (range, cone, silence, recharge).
  • Tap a step to preview it: the clip/diagram/bars switch to that step and the numbers that go up turn yellow, like Gunsmith's green/red stat changes.
